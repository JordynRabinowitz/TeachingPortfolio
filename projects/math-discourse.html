Building independent math discourse

An ongoing inquiry into how structured discussion and gradually reduced scaffolding can support students in expressing their mathematical reasoning.

Inquiry researchAcademic discourseUDLFormative assessment
The context

Academic discourse is a schoolwide focus at Marco Forster Middle School, where I have had professional development opportunities centered on strengthening student discussion. That learning connects directly to my culminating inquiry research project for my MAT program at UC Irvine.

The design question

Working inquiry question: How can structured student discussions help learners communicate their mathematical reasoning with increasing independence? This is a working framing, not a finalized research question.

What I designed and contributed

  • Build an independent think-and-write step before asking students to share with a partner.
  • Use vocabulary banks, sentence stems, and rotating discussion roles to help students enter the conversation.
  • Circulate during the think phase and listen during partner work to distinguish conceptual confusion from language or confidence barriers.
  • Gradually move from highly supported explanations toward students writing and saying fuller, independent mathematical justifications.

Conversation is not automatically meaningful just because students are grouped together. Structure can provide access, while planned release of that structure creates space for student ownership of the reasoning.

Where this work stands

Ongoing research. No findings or impact claims are presented here yet.
