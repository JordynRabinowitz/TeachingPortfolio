Discovering angle relationships

An inquiry-based lesson that helps students discover congruent and supplementary angle relationships through movement, conversation, and visual models.

Math 8GeometryHands-on investigationUDL
The context

Rather than begin by asking students to memorize angle rules, I designed a lesson in which students can first see and test the relationships for themselves. The lesson uses parallel lines and a transversal laid out with tape, along with pre-cut paper angles labeled A and B.

The design question

How can students develop an intuitive understanding of angle relationships before attaching formal mathematical vocabulary?

What I designed and contributed

  • Prepared tape models and movable paper angle pieces so students can compare angle measures physically.
  • Started with prior knowledge of straight angles and 180°, then prompted students to notice patterns and justify what they found.
  • Used projected directions, vocabulary references in English and Spanish, and optional sentence frames to support access.
  • Built in partner conversation before individual diagrams and written explanation.

The tactile model reduces the distance between an abstract geometry diagram and what students can actually observe. Asking students to explain a pattern, not only name it, gives me formative evidence of their reasoning.

Where this work stands

Lesson plan and instructional materials developed in fall 2026. This page emphasizes instructional design rather than claiming measured outcomes.
