My approach to teaching

Finding solutions starts with asking the right questions.

A philosophy grounded in coaching, accessible math instruction, student discourse, and learning through making.

I see myself as a coach rather than someone who simply gives students the answers. My role as an educator is to give students the tools, skills, and encouragement they need to approach problems independently. I believe challenging math should be accessible to all students without lowering expectations. Ultimately, I want students to develop the confidence to tackle unfamiliar problems, communicate their thinking, and recognize that mistakes are part of learning.

To me, success in math goes beyond finding the correct answer. Students should understand why a solution works and be able to explain their reasoning. Academic discourse is a schoolwide focus at Marco Forster Middle School, where I have had opportunities to participate in professional development focused on strengthening student discussions. This has also become the focus of my master's culminating inquiry research project, in which I am exploring how structured discussions can support students in communicating their math reasoning. In my classroom, I use independent thinking time, rotating group roles, sentence stems, vocabulary supports, and peer conversations to help students develop and share their ideas. My goal is to gradually remove these scaffolds so students become more confident explaining their reasoning independently.

My background in Creative Technology and Design has also shaped how I approach teaching. I believe students learn through making, experimenting, and reflecting on their work. By balancing clear instruction with collaborative exploration and student choice, I want to create opportunities for students to apply what they learn to meaningful problems.

As technology continues to evolve, I believe education should prepare students to do more than simply use the tools available to them. Students should learn to question those tools, evaluate the solutions they produce, and eventually create solutions of their own. Whether through math, engineering, or computer science, I want students to recognize that they have the ability to contribute to and shape our future.
